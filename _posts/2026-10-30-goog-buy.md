### Search: this is a monetization problem, not a demand problem

The bear case says AI is eating search. The usage data does not support that. Year-over-year query growth is the **highest in five years**. AI Mode queries run ~3x longer than traditional searches; one in five AI Mode searches globally uses voice, images or documents; visual search crossed 50B queries a month. Schindler's most important sentence was that **the majority of AI Mode queries are incremental** — new demand, not cannibalized classic search — and they skew to upper-funnel, commercial intent. Sponsored click-through rates in AI Mode are already roughly equal to organic product-list CTR.

What broke in Q3 was revenue *per* query. Search grew 12%, down from 17% in Q2, and management decomposed the deceleration into four parts: **FX was the single largest driver** (headwinds concentrated in Search and YouTube ads), the EU's removal of the de-minimis exemption hurt APAC retail advertisers, media spend normalized after a World Cup-boosted June, and **AI Mode monetizes less than classic search**. Ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode are only now scaling internationally, alongside US multi-turn ads.

The offsetting pull is ad quality. Gemini generated **1.5 billion campaign assets in Q3, up more than 7x year over year**; Discover ads delivered 18% incremental advertiser value; Telstra saw a 52% conversion lift and 36% lower cost per order through AI Max. Better models raise advertiser ROI, which is a pricing-power argument for the auction independent of query volume.

Q4 is guided to look worse on the same lines: a slightly larger FX headwind, and roughly two points of growth lapping as the Q3'25 search acceleration rolls off.

### Subscriptions and YouTube: the hedge against ad cyclicality

$14.3B (+11%), and subscriptions are now growing faster than ads. YouTube Music and Premium passed **150 million subscribers** (including trials), Premium Lite is a deliberate price-ladder for volume, and **Google One AI plans are Alphabet's fastest-growing subscription product**. YouTube ads grew 9% to $11.21B, with living room now accounting for over a third of YouTube ad revenue, 20M+ videos uploaded daily, 1.3M+ channels on YouTube Shopping and Amazon joining the platform. Consumer AI at Google does not have to be monetized through ads — it can be sold into existing subscriptions at $20–$250 per month tiers, through a Gemini app that crossed 1 billion monthly active users (up from 950M in August), the fastest-growing product in the company's history. **Gemini app revenue is not disclosed anywhere**, which makes it the largest unpriced line in the story.

### Google Cloud: the part of the business that genuinely changed

$30.71B (+103%), operating income tripled to $11.12B, margin 36.2%, and a **$561B backlog** — up from $514B last quarter — with roughly 55% (~$308B) scheduled for recognition within 24 months. That is about two quarters of revenue already contracted.

Inside it: GCP is the vast majority of revenue, and management said growth excluding TPU system sales was "again very, very strong" (Kurian declined to quantify it — the number investors want and won't get). The TPU platform has become a real revenue line: 8t for training at 2.7x better training performance and 8i for inference at 80% better price-performance; more than 100 significant TPU customers; delivery partnerships with **Crux AI and Oracle** into third-party data centers; **Apple scaling pretraining on Google TPUs**; frontier labs (Mirendil, Physical Intelligence); Citadel Securities and Jump Trading. Gemini Enterprise sits on top of it: ~90% of the Fortune 100 use it, customers who used it in Q3'25 **quadrupled their spend** year over year, more than 100 million seats have been sold for knowledge work, and 2,600 customers have consumed at least 100B tokens. The AI flywheel is multi-line — using Gemini drives compute, storage for agent memory, database for context, and cybersecurity.

Two caveats: TPU system sales are hardware-flavored (lower margin, working-capital heavy, NVIDIA-competitive) and they flatter the optics; and Cloud margin faces guided near-term headwinds from third-party bridging capacity that came online only at the end of Q3.

### The model layer: Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 4 Carbon

Two frontier releases in four weeks is the cadence of a company that believes it is winning — and it is the most important new variable in the business, because model capability is the upstream input to every revenue line above.

**Gemini 4 Argon** (announced Sept 30, 2026) was built for long-horizon software engineering, enterprise knowledge work and cyber defense: 1M-token context, up to 1M output tokens, introductory pricing of **$2/$10 per million input/output tokens** with cached input at 95% off — half the price of Claude Opus 5.5. It posted the highest or tied score in 13 of 18 categories on Google's own benchmark table, ranked #1 on LMArena as of October 15 (1524), #1 of 45 on IOI, and #3 of 27 on the independent Vals Index (69.24%). It is not, however, the best at everything: it trails Claude Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (57.4% vs 66.4%) and GPT-6 Astra on FrontierSWE v2 (55% vs 65.5%). Access was deliberately gated to trusted cyber defenders through the Fairwind program.

**Gemini 4 Carbon**, launched hours before the Q3 print, is Google's most capable model: **#1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 with 59, one point ahead of Claude Opus 5.5**; new state of the art in cybersecurity with 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 71% on CWE-bench and 54.8% on ExploitGym; more than double Argon's success rate at generating vulnerability-free code; Google's Chrome team reported it producing high-quality security patches more than 10x better than an unnamed rival; and a 89-point WebDev Arena gain to 1,768. It launches at the same $2/$10 introductory price with the same 95% cached-input discount.

Three caveats on the model story. First, **leadership has decayed into a commodity**: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5/Sonnet 5.5/Fable 5.1, Meta's Muse and Spark, DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash — a frontier advantage measured in points on an index is a marketing asset with a shelf life of weeks, so durable value has to accrue to distribution and ads, not to API margin. Second, **the cost of serving is about to step up**: introductory pricing on Argon, Carbon and Flash expires between November 30 and December 31, 2026, and Carbon's measured time-to-first-token of roughly 100 seconds is slow for interactive consumer use — which is exactly why Pichai said Google is building distilled versions of Carbon to run at 1-billion-user scale. Third, **Carbon was access-gated on day one**, so the second model of the quarter contributed essentially nothing to Q3 revenue; it is a Q4'26/2027 story.

Org matters here too. In August 2026, Demis Hassabis moved to Chair of Google DeepMind and Alphabet Chief Scientist (keeping Isomorphic Labs), Koray Kavukcuoglu rose to SVP Google DeepMind, and **Jeff Dean left after 27 years** to found Discovery Loop with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le — with Google as founding investor and cloud partner. Losing a quarter of the leadership bench that built Google's search and ML infrastructure is a genuine risk to a model lead that only shows up as slowed cadence or missed launches. Offsetting it: Gemma has passed 900M downloads, Google's Cyber model is already live and selling, and the release cadence since August (Argon, then Carbon) has not visibly slipped.

### Two items hiding behind the earnings

**The equity portfolio is a mark-to-market lottery bolted to EPS.** Cash and marketable securities are $265.1B, including **$90.4B of marketable equity securities**; the SpaceX stake alone swung +$99B of "revenue" in Q2 and **‑$8.7B in Q3**. The mitigating arithmetic: Alphabet's pre-AI ROIC was above 30% against a ~10.5% cost of capital, so even a mediocre return on $300B a year of AI spend clears the hurdle that would sink a lower-return business.

### What the business says about the thesis

The operating business is stronger than the quarter's optics: revenue at a half-trillion run rate, Cloud doubling with $561B contracted, subscriptions outgrowing ads, Gemini at a billion users, and query demand at a five-year high. What broke the stock was not demand — it was search monetization growing slower than queries, a cash flow statement that turned negative because the buildout is funded with debt, contracts and equity issuance, and an earnings line polluted by a SpaceX mark.

The models are the hinge. Argon and Carbon put Google at or near the frontier of intelligence, and frontier capability is what converts a $30B Cloud business into a compounding one, a 1-billion-user Gemini funnel into a subscription business, and AI Mode from a monetization drag into a defensible surface. But the market will now demand the proof in a currency it hasn't seen yet: Cloud revenue ex-TPU, Gemini app revenue, AI-plan subscriptions, and revenue per AI Mode query. Carbon launched two hours before the print with access restricted to a handful of cyber defenders, so the model story and the revenue story are running about two quarters apart — and it is that gap, not the benchmarks, that determines whether Alphabet is cheap at ~20x forward earnings excluding investment marks.
